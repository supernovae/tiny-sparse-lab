# DevMind v3: independent engineering-rich source candidate

**Protocol ID:** `devmind-v3-engineering-prospective-1` (2026-09-29). This is a new candidate in `corpora/devmind-v3/` with its own workspace, acquisition lock, build, and frozen release. Its 19 v2 foundation source declarations are byte-identical copies, so source/revision/rights decisions and the four existing held-out families retain their identity. Verified source snapshots can be copied using filesystem copy-on-write and independently checked; do not edit the frozen v2 release, source snapshots, failed stages, or evidence. Do not fit a tokenizer, train a model, or claim model quality in this campaign.

## Source intent and quality

Select pinned first-party authored implementation, focused tests, diagnostics/error handling, design/proposal and operator docs, examples, protocol/specification and schema/config/build files across OS, storage, containers, networking, cloud/distributed systems, observability, security, language runtimes, and developer tooling. Explicit path inclusion, file-level rights review, and exclusion of third-party/vendor/generated/large fixture/private material matter more than gross upstream repository size. Record acquisition size by source, its authored shapes/languages and retention after exact/canonical dedup; duplicated upstream mirrors contribute only one retained text. A permissive license is not a proxy for educational quality. Maintain human-readable per-source selection decisions and pin/license evidence in source declarations and the [source research](source-research.md). Never assume all files in a repository have its root SPDX license; preserve file-level overrides and exclude unknown restrictions.

Do not expand the existing generic-web, Wikipedia, mathematics or academic shards to manufacture developer share. First-party manuals and standards are useful for invariant- and operator-oriented reasoning, not a substitute for implementation/tests. Reassess dataset/index proposals from primary terms and demonstrably materializable pinned shards; a code-index entry without licensed accessible bytes counts as zero.

## Academic title screen before engineering expansion

The existing v2 train has **254,968 peS2o abstracts / 337,471,617 retained UTF-8 bytes** and **25,762 full texts / 717,323,535 retained UTF-8 bytes**. The pinned peS2o snapshots provide paper ID, source row and authored text but no trustworthy subject field. The normalized `title` field is **the input shard filename**, not the paper title; the authored first line of `text` is the usable title. [`classify_pes2o.py`](classify_pes2o.py) streams the frozen v2 release and applies versioned topical expressions **only to this first line**. The report [`pes2o-incidence.json`](pes2o-incidence.json) records per-topic overlapping incidence, unique-any-match incidence, retained text bytes and deterministic review titles. A match is an approximate topical signal, **not a validated developer document**, a licensing decision, or a new `developer_systems` domain; unmatched papers may still be software relevant, and ambiguous terms ("operating system", "distributed system") can produce false positives. Keep the paper sources `academic_research` and their split unchanged. Never count the entire academic pool toward unique first-party developer supply.

The first-line screen matches **713/254,968 abstract titles** (833,073/337,471,617 retained bytes) and **31/25,762 full-text titles** (847,781/717,323,535 retained bytes), across partly overlapping programming, software engineering, OS, distributed/networking and data/security expressions. The deterministic samples include false positives such as an electrical generator \"operating system\". Keep all of these papers academically labeled.

## Decision rule

Report retained unique developer and nondeveloper train documents/bytes, coverage per requested vertical and per language/shape, rights and exclusions, split families, dropped duplicate groups, and selected-vs-retained bytes by source. `measure_pool.py` reports byte-proxy developer exposure to reach descriptive **20%, 30%, 40%** share as `p × nondeveloper_bytes / ((1−p) × developer_bytes)` passes relative to one nondeveloper pass. This is **not** a chosen training mixture, tokenizer token counts, or evidence of sampling quality; if repetition is high, the decision must say `EXPAND_MORE`. Use frozen release `report.json`, `audit.json`, receipts and progress for source counts, bytes, phase timing, observed RSS/disk and incomplete near-screen limitations. Prefer ~500 MB or more distinct developer/systems train bytes and credible vertical/language breadth before tokenizer readiness. If measured supply, heldout isolation, source terms or scalable near review fall short, decide **`EXPAND_MORE`**, not an optimistic readiness label. Copyleft obligations or the separate unresolved model-weight-license question are documented but are **not** by themselves a private tokenizer-readiness veto; explicit incompatible ML-training restrictions are.

Before long acquisition/build, inspect available bytes/inodes and a conservative cache/snapshot/build/release growth budget on the actual `sparselab-work/experiments/devmind-pretrain-v3/` filesystem. Build progress must record stage counters and RSS; preserve interruption evidence. Freeze only a verified immutable v3 build. The release/report is evidence of corpus construction, not a scientific model-quality result.

## Decision: EXPAND_MORE

At one pass through the **3,264,104,346-byte nondeveloper** train pool, byte-proxy developer exposure of **20% / 30% / 40%** requires **1.597 / 2.738 / 4.260 passes** over the **510,860,750-byte distinct developer** pool. The measured pool is more than ten times the v2 developer pool and contains real implementation and tests across OS, container/network, distributed storage, security, observability, languages and build systems. But 40% would repeat it over four times; SQL/JavaScript train content is absent, shell/config/build/operator/example material is thin relative to code, and independent config/operations heldout cases are thin. These are **source-coverage gaps, not tokenizer or model test results**. A next separately versioned candidate should add independently authored, rights-reviewed SQL, shell, operational configs/runbooks, schemas/build examples and whole-source config/operator heldouts; keep existing v2/v3 releases immutable, eliminate license-boilerplate overlap as appropriate, and rerun source and heldout audits. The choice does not silently prescribe a training mixture or turn copyleft/weight-license analysis into this private-source readiness gate.
